1 · The market need

Pharma companies no longer ask whether to use real-world evidence. They ask whether a regulator, a payer or their own governance board will accept it. The answer depends less on the platform and more on whether the study can show how it was run.

Demand
96%

of biopharma leaders call real-world data and evidence essential to strategy. 80% expect generative AI to change how evidence is produced within a year.

Deloitte RWE benchmark, 2025

Acceptance
15%

of the real-world studies in 88 FDA approvals were judged inadequate. The reasons were study design and data relevance: confounding, selection bias, misclassification, missing data.

Purpura et al., Clin Pharmacol Ther, 2022

Reproducibility
7%

of 250 published studies referenced their analysis code. Nine expert teams given the same data and written methods built cohorts from 2,159 to 63,619 patients.

Wang et al., 2022; Ostropolets et al., 2023

Three pressures are converging

  • Regulators now ask for the same study record on both sides of the Atlantic. ICH M14 has applied in the EU since March 2026 and is final at FDA. With FDA's RWE guidances and EMA's 2025 reflection paper, it asks for a protocol fixed before the outcome analysis, a data source shown fit for the question, validated definitions, and traceability from source data to result.
  • Access to data is becoming conditional on governance. From March 2029 the European Health Data Space gives access through a data permit, for a stated purpose, inside a secure environment. In the US, data-owner agreements must already allow FDA to inspect the source.
  • AI raises the stakes. Language models asked for clinical code lists returned about a third invented codes in one study. FDA and EMA published joint principles in January 2026 that ask for documented, human-checked use.

What buyers say is hard

In surveys, the top barriers are data compatibility (29%), privacy and security (27%), scarce talent that combines evidence and AI skills (25%) and regulatory uncertainty (23%). The last three figures come from a trade-press summary of Deloitte's survey. Fragmented tools make it hard to show the return on the investment.

2 · The opportunity

Audit trails, role-based access and security certificates are standard: every serious RWE platform claims them. What we did not find in any vendor's public material is one record that joins the steps a study goes through. This is an absence in public material, not proof that nobody does it.

Why Dataiku. The suite already runs across the customer's own data platforms (Snowflake, Databricks, Redshift) on the OMOP standard, and Dataiku Govern provides workflows, sign-offs and deployment gates. Purpose-built RWE tools govern inside their own product, and data science platforms govern models. Governing a study across tools is the open position.

How we describe it. The suite demonstrates controls that support regulated work; the customer validates its own system. Comparable platforms use the same wording, and Dataiku's own material does not claim Part 11 compliance.

5 · Proposed MVP scope

The MVP is done when

  • The demo study walks all six sign-offs in Govern, each linked to its evidence.
  • The outcome analysis refuses to run when the protocol file is changed after lock.
  • A changed data cut or vocabulary version marks the downstream approvals stale.
  • The automation node refuses an unapproved release and accepts the approved one.
  • The repository check fails on unexpected access, a missing record, an unpinned version or a missing sign-off.

7 · User stories

User stories are written once the scope in section 5 is agreed. They will be grouped by the stakeholder who acts, one set per process step:

  • Data owner: accept a dataset and state its permitted uses.
  • Clinical researcher and informaticist: review and approve a cohort; see what changed since the last approval.
  • Epidemiologist and biostatistician: record data fitness; lock a protocol; review diagnostics.
  • Study owner and suite owner: approve a study and a release; see what is stale after a refresh.
  • QA and compliance: retrieve the full record for one result.

What the migration should leave behind

No new migration work, only records that are cheap to keep now and hard to recover later:

  • The data cut and source of DEMO_SF_PREP_OMOP_2M3 when it is created, and the OMOP vocabulary version if the source carries it. The 15 export tables do not include the tables where that version normally lives; whether the source has them is not checked.
  • The cohort counts DEMO_DB_RWD2 recorded on 2025-10-06, exported before it is retired. They become the reference for the cohort regression check.
  • The cross-database test result for the three sample cohorts. It is the evidence for sign-off 2.
  • The notebook baseline of the Cox results. It becomes the study's first reference result.

Appendix · Technical specification

For the builders. Step numbers G1–G6 are the six process steps above.

A · Principles
  1. Govern the study, not only the platform. Each platform control is tied to the study record it protects.
  2. A sign-off is backed by evidence. The step's required fields are the evidence: an index row, a DSS check result, a file hash or a bundle.
  3. The executable specification is the record. Protocol, cohort definitions and code, pinned to a data cut and a vocabulary version.
  4. Reuse the framework. Fork versions of existing blueprints. Two blueprints are new: the cohort definition and the study.
  5. Governance configuration is code. Blueprint versions, sign-off configs and the role map are exported into the repo and imported to Govern as DRAFT. Reviewers are always Govern roles.
  6. Scale through templates. A suite tag selects projects for Project Standards and for Govern.
  7. Writes to Govern follow the wiki rule. A drift report is the default; pushing records needs the owner's approval.

H · Out of scope and technical risks
  • Electronic-signature integration (Part 11 Subpart C), IQ/OQ/PQ, any claim of a validated system.
  • User Isolation Framework, per-user database credentials, audit-log dispatch to an external store.
  • Running a phenotype validation or a real fitness verdict; negative controls unless decided in.
  • EHDS secure-environment behaviour and actual catalogue registration; packaging patient-level data for submission.
